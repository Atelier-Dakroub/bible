# Study

This page tells you about the study tools in the search panel: word studies, people and places, the passage guide, cross-references, quotations, topics, the landscape and Books.

## Word study

Tap a word on a passage card. Or search for a Strong's number, such as `H2617`, or a word of the original, such as `chesed`. The search panel shows the word study:

- The Hebrew or Greek word, its transliteration, Strong's pronunciation, its number and the number of verses that use it.
- A speaker button beside the word. Tap it to hear the word in the device's Hebrew or Greek voice.
- The definition from Strong's dictionary, and the words by which the KJV renders it.
- **Senses**: the senses of the word, each with its domain, a short definition and the number of verses. Tap a sense to list only its verses. Tap it again to list all the verses.
- How often the KJV renders the word by each English word, and a bar for each book where the word occurs.
- The verses that use the word.

While a word study is on show, every word on the table that renders the same original word is underlined. This shows how the original and the KJV line up.

The buttons of a word study:

- **Mark it on every card** marks the word on every card, also on cards that you put down later. See [Tools](/guide/tools#marks).
- **Where, chapter by chapter** puts the landscape of the word on the table.
- **Cross-references of** the verse lists the cross-references of the verse that you tapped.

## People, places, groups and events

A word that names a person, place or group has a dotted underline. Tap it. The search panel shows the entry:

- Its facts, such as dates and family.
- A picture, if the app has one. Tap a picture to put it on the table.
- The text of Easton's Bible Dictionary. A long entry stops after a few sentences. Tap **Read more** for the rest.
- Its relations, as buttons. Tap one to show that person, place or event.
- **Often named with**: the names that are most often in the same verses.
- The verses that name it.

The references in the text of an entry are links. Tap a reference to put the verse on the table. Or drag it out to the side, as you drag a result.

The buttons of an entry:

- **Show on the map** marks the place on the map, and zooms the map to it.
- **Mark it on every card** marks every word that names it, on every card.
- **Where, chapter by chapter** puts its landscape on the table.

To put the entry on the table as a card, drag its heading out of the panel. A name of a people that has no entry of its own, such as "Hittite", shows its entry in Easton's dictionary.

An entry of a person can also list **Journeys on the map**. See [The map](/guide/map#journeys).

## The passage guide

Tap the heading of a passage card. Or give the card the focus and press <kbd>Enter</kbd>. The search panel shows the guide to the passage. It can have these parts:

- The notes on the table that quote the passage. Tap one to bring it into view.
- Pictures of the places and people that the passage names.
- **Video**: the videos of the passage.
- **Parallel passages**: other passages that tell the same thing, verse after verse in the same order. For example, Kings and Chronicles, or the Gospels.
- The quotations: **The Old Testament it quotes**, or **Quoted in the New Testament**.
- **Commentaries**: what each commentary says on the passage, with its first words. Tap one to put it on the table as a card. See [Commentaries](#commentaries).
- **Cross-references**: the references of all its verses together, most voted first.
- **People**, **Places**, **Groups** and **Topics** that the passage names, as buttons.
- **Events, on the timeline**, with their dates.
- **Journeys on the map**, for a passage that tells part of a journey.

The buttons of the guide:

- **Start a study** lays out a study of the passage on the table. See [Start a study](#start-a-study).
- **Put them beside the card** puts the parallel passages in a row to the right of the card, at the same size. Undo takes them back in one step.
- **Show its places on the map** marks all its places on the map.

## Start a study

**Start a study**, at the top of a passage guide, puts a first study of the passage on the table, in columns:

| Column | What it holds |
| --- | --- |
| Left | The Old Testament passages that the passage quotes, for a New Testament passage. |
| Middle | The passage, a study note, and the people the passage names. |
| Next | The New Testament passages that quote it, for an Old Testament passage. Then its parallel passages. |
| Right | The five cross-references with the most votes. |
| Last | The map, with the passage's places marked, if the map is not on the table yet. |

The study goes in the open table to the right of your cards, and the table moves to show it. If the study is wider than the screen, the table zooms out so that more of it fits, but not below 60%, where the words are still easy to read.

- **The study note** says what the study holds, and has three headings for you to write under: Observations, Questions and Application.
- **Every card is linked to the passage.** A quotation is a "quotes" link, from the New Testament to the Old. So the whole study moves as one group (see [Move linked cards together](/guide/cards#move-linked-cards-together)).
- **Each verse comes once.** A cross-reference that shares verses with a card in the study is left out. People named all through the Bible, such as God and Jesus, get no card.
- **One Undo takes the whole study away.**

**Start a study** is also on a word study, a topic, and a person, place, event or group:

| Start a study on | The middle | Beside it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A word study (a Strong's number) | The word's landscape (where it is used) and the note | The first verse for each sense of the word, then the first verse in each book that uses it most. Every card marks the word. |
| A topic of Nave's | The note, which lists the parts of the topic | A passage for each part of the topic, the first one it cites |
| A person | The person, the note, and their family | The chapters that name them most. The map shows their first journey, or where they were born and died. |
| A place | The place, the note, and the first events there, or the people born or who died there | The chapters that name it most. The map shows the place. The note lists every event there, and who was born and who died there. |
| An event | The event, the note, and the people in it | The passages that tell it, in canon order. The map shows its places. The note gives its date, what it is part of, and the events within it. |
| A group | The group and the note | Its members named most, ten at most, then the chapters that name the group, where any do. |

A group that nothing names and that has no members, such as the Pharisees, has no study.

The study comes from the app's own data, the same as the guide. No language model writes it. To have Claude add notes of its own, see [The assistant](/guide/assistant).

The guide finds a parallel only where many verses refer to each other in order. It does not find a quotation of one verse in this way. The quotations have their own part of the guide.

## Commentaries

Five old commentaries come with the app. All are in the public domain:

| Commentary | Covers |
| --- | --- |
| **Matthew Henry**, Concise Commentary (1706–1721) | The whole Bible, a section of verses at a time |
| **Jamieson-Fausset-Brown** (1871) | The whole Bible, verse by verse, with notes on the Hebrew and Greek |
| **Wesley**, Explanatory Notes (1754–1765) | Almost the whole Bible, in short notes |
| **Geneva notes** (1599) | Most of the Bible: the verse, then its notes, as the Geneva Bible prints them |
| **Catena Aurea**, Thomas Aquinas (1842 translation) | The four Gospels, in the words of the Church Fathers |

To read one, open the passage guide and tap the commentary under **Commentaries**. Or drag it out to the side to put it where you want.

On a commentary card:

- Each comment begins with its verses, such as "Verses 1–8". A commentary that writes on a section of verses shows the section that the passage is in.
- A long comment comes one page at a time. Tap **›** for the next page, then the next comment, and **‹** to go back.
- A reference in the comment puts that passage beside the card.
- **Sync** joins the card to synced passage cards. When the passage steps, the commentary goes to the same passage. When the commentary steps to the next comment, the passage goes to that comment's verses.

## Cross-references

To see the cross-references of a verse:

- Tap a word with no Strong's number, or tap between the words.
- Or tap **Cross-references of** in a word study.

The panel lists the references, most voted first. Put them on the table as you put any result.

When a verse on one card refers to a verse on another card, a dotted gray curve joins the two cards. It leaves each card at its edge, level with the verse, and goes under the cards, so it does not cover the text.

## The Old Testament in the New

A lapis quotation mark in the right margin of a card marks a quotation:

- On a New Testament verse, where a quotation of the Old Testament begins.
- On an Old Testament verse that the New Testament quotes.

Tap the mark. The search panel lists the other side. The passage guide also has a part for the quotations. The app marks quotations, not allusions.

## Topics

The topics come from Nave's Topical Bible.

- Search for a topic, such as `prayer`. The topic shows above the verses. Tap it to open it.
- The passage guide lists the topics that cite the passage, the topics that cite it most first. Tap one to open it.

An open topic shows each heading with its references. Tap a reference to put the passage on the table. Tap a "See" link to go to another topic.

## The landscape

**Where, chapter by chapter** puts a landscape card on the table. You find this button in a word study, in an entry of a person or place, and under the results of a search for words or grammar.

The landscape has one cell for each chapter of the Bible. A cell is larger where the subject occurs more often.

- Tap a chapter. The chapter goes on the table beside the landscape.
- Tap the heading of the landscape to show its subject in the search panel again.

## Books

Tap **Books** on the rail. A card compares the 66 books. Each book is a bar as long as its verses, cut into its chapters. The card also shows the group of each book and the author that tradition gives it. Tap a chapter to put it on the table beside the card. Tap **Books** again to take the card away.
