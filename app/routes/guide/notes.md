# Notes

This page tells you how to write a note on the table, how to add references and quotes, how to format a note, and how note kinds and questions work.

## A new note

Tap **Note** on the rail. A note card goes on the table, and you can write in it at once.

## Write in a note

You write on the note card itself, where it lies on the table.

- Tap the text of a note. The caret goes where you tapped.
- To stop writing, tap anywhere off the note, or press <kbd>Escape</kbd>.

On a large touch screen with no keyboard, the app's own keyboard shows under the note, with a bar that says "Writing a note". Tap **Done** on the bar to stop writing. The **Hide** key of the keyboard hides the keyboard, and the note stays open. See [Keys and touch](/guide/keys-touch#the-app-s-on-screen-keyboard).

The table keeps your words as you write. Undo takes back the whole edit of a note as one step, after you stop writing.

## The title

Tap the heading of a note to write its title. Press <kbd>Enter</kbd>, or tap somewhere else, to keep the title. Press <kbd>Escape</kbd> to keep the title that was there before. A note with no title shows the name of its kind as its heading.

## References

A reference in a note is a button. When you do not write in the note, a tap on the reference does this:

- A reference to a passage puts the passage on the table beside the note.
- A reference to a person, place, group or event puts its card on the table beside the note.
- A reference to a word of the original opens its word study in the search panel.

### Type a reference

Type a reference, such as `John 3:16` or `Jn 3`. A suggestion shows the passage and its first words.

- Press <kbd>Tab</kbd>, or tap the suggestion, to make it a reference.
- <kbd>Enter</kbd> still starts a new line.

### Use @

Type <kbd>@</kbd> and the start of a name, such as `@abra`, `@H430` or `@agape`. The suggestions show people, places, groups, events and words of the original.

- Press <kbd>↑</kbd> and <kbd>↓</kbd> to choose a suggestion.
- Press <kbd>Enter</kbd> or <kbd>Tab</kbd>, or tap a suggestion, to make it a reference.
- Press <kbd>Escape</kbd> to put the suggestions away until the words change.

## Quotes

A quote is the words of a passage in a note. Its words keep their place in the Bible. A dashed line joins the quote to the highlight that it came from. On the other cards, a verse that a note quotes has a gray line down its left side.

There are two ways to put a quote into a note:

1. Mark words on a card with two hands, as for a highlight. Do not lift the marking finger. Drag it onto the note and lift it there.
2. Write a reference to verses in the note. Select the reference, then tap **Quote the verses** on the format bar. The reference opens into the words of the verses.

At the top of each quote are two buttons:

- The fold button (**Make it a reference**) folds the quote back into a reference.
- **×** removes the quote.

## The format bar

Select words in a note, with a finger or with the mouse. A bar shows near the words. A pressed button shows a format that the words have now. Press it again to take the format off.

| Button | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **Quote the verses** | Opens a selected reference into the words of its verses. It shows only for a reference. |
| **Bold** | Makes the words bold. |
| **Italic** | Makes the words italic. |
| **Heading** | Makes the line a heading. |
| **List** | Makes the line an item of a list. |
| **Numbered list** | Makes the line an item of a numbered list. |
| **Highlight in ochre**, **lapis** or **verdigris** | Highlights the words in that color. |

### Keys for formatting

| Keys | What they do |
| --- | --- |
| <kbd>Command</kbd> <kbd>B</kbd> (on a Mac) or <kbd>Control</kbd> <kbd>B</kbd> | Bold |
| <kbd>Command</kbd> <kbd>I</kbd> or <kbd>Control</kbd> <kbd>I</kbd> | Italic |
| `#` and a space, at the start of a line | A heading |
| `##` and a space, at the start of a line | A smaller heading |
| `-` and a space, at the start of a line | A list |
| `1.` and a space, at the start of a line | A numbered list |
| <kbd>Tab</kbd> and <kbd>Shift</kbd> <kbd>Tab</kbd>, in a list | Moves the item in and out |
| <kbd>Alt</kbd> <kbd>F10</kbd> (<kbd>Option</kbd> <kbd>F10</kbd> on a Mac), with words selected | Goes into the format bar. <kbd>←</kbd> and <kbd>→</kbd> move along it, <kbd>Enter</kbd> formats the words, and <kbd>Escape</kbd> goes back to the words. |
| <kbd>Command</kbd> <kbd>Z</kbd> or <kbd>Control</kbd> <kbd>Z</kbd> | Undoes your typing in the note |
| <kbd>Shift</kbd> <kbd>Command</kbd> <kbd>Z</kbd> or <kbd>Shift</kbd> <kbd>Control</kbd> <kbd>Z</kbd> (also <kbd>Command</kbd> <kbd>Y</kbd> or <kbd>Control</kbd> <kbd>Y</kbd>) | Redoes your typing in the note |

## Note kinds

A label at the top of each note shows its kind. At first it is **Note**. Tap the label to go to the next kind, in this order:

1. **Observation**: what the text says.
2. **Question**: what you ask of the text.
3. **Interpretation**: what the text means.
4. **Application**: what the text asks of you.
5. **Prayer**.

After **Prayer**, a tap goes back to **Note**. Each kind has its own color on the card.

## Questions

A **Question** note has an **Answered** box at the top. Tick it when you have the answer.

When the table has questions, a **?** button shows on the research trail of the search panel, with the number of questions. Tap it to list them: the open questions first, then the answered ones. Tap a question to bring its note into view.

## Notes in the passage guide

The guide to a passage lists the notes on the table that quote it. Tap one to bring it into view. See [Study](/guide/study#the-passage-guide).

## Notes as Markdown

You can take your notes out of the app as Markdown text:

- **One note**: tap the copy button at the top of the note. The note goes to the clipboard as Markdown, with its title.
- **All the notes**: in **Tools**, **Saved**, tap **Notes as Markdown**. The app saves a file with all the notes of the table, grouped by kind. A question says if it is answered.

In the Markdown, each reference is a link to the app, and each quote is a block quote with its reference and version.
