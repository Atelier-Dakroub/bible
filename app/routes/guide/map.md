# The map

This page tells you about the map card: how to move and zoom it, the places it names, the ancient world, the journeys and the scale.

## The map card

Tap **Map** on the rail. A map of the biblical world goes on the table, from Italy to Persia. Tap **Map** again to take it away.

Every place with known coordinates is a dot. A mountain has a peak in place of a dot. The names of the mountain ranges, seas, gulfs and lakes are on the map. Where the Bible gives a sea its own name, that name is under the modern name, in the words of the KJV. For example, the Mediterranean is "the Great Sea".

The coastlines are the modern ones. In some places the ancient coast was different, for example at the head of the Persian Gulf. To see the shores of an ancient age, see [The ancient world](#the-ancient-world).

## Move and zoom the map

The map moves and zooms inside its frame. The card stays where it is. To move the card, drag its heading or the credit at the bottom.

| Inside the frame | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| One finger, or a mouse drag | Moves the map |
| Two fingers | Zoom the map about their middle, up to 16 times |
| The wheel, or two fingers on a trackpad | Move the map |
| A pinch on the trackpad, or <kbd>Control</kbd> or <kbd>Command</kbd> and the wheel | Zooms the map about the pointer |
| **+** and **−** in the corner | Zoom in and out about the middle |
| **Whole map** in the corner | Shows all of the map |

With the keyboard, move the focus to the frame of the map with <kbd>Tab</kbd>. Then:

- The arrow keys move the map.
- <kbd>+</kbd> and <kbd>-</kbd> zoom in and out.
- <kbd>0</kbd> shows the whole map.

At the **Far** size, press <kbd>Enter</kbd> on the map card to step into the map. Press <kbd>Escape</kbd> to step out again. See [Keys and touch](/guide/keys-touch#the-wall).

The table keeps the view of the map. A move of the map is not a change for Undo.

## Names and zoom

At the whole map, only the places that the Bible names most often have their names. Each closer level (2, 4, 8 and 16 times) names more places, where there is room. Names never cover each other. At the closest level, every place shows.

The dots, names and lines keep their size at every zoom. Only the distances grow.

## Places named by the cards

The places that the cards on the table name always have their names on the map. A dashed line joins each place to each card that names it. A line goes only to a place inside the view of the map.

- Tap a place on the map to show it in the search panel.
- Tap the name of a sea or a lake to show it in the search panel.
- In an entry of a place, tap **Show on the map**. The map marks the place and zooms to it.
- In a passage guide, tap **Show its places on the map**. The map marks all the places of the passage and zooms to fit them.

When the map is not on the table, these buttons put it there.

## The ancient world

Under the zoom buttons is the **Show on the map** list. Choose one layer to put over today's map:

| Choice | What it shows |
| --- | --- |
| **Today's map** | No layer |
| **The Persian Empire, about 500 BC** | Its extent |
| **The empire of Alexander, 323 BC** | Its extent |
| **The Hasmonean kingdom, at its widest, about 76 BC** | Its extent |
| **Rome, 60 BC** | Its extent |
| **Herod's kingdom, at his death, 4 BC** | Its extent |
| **The Roman Empire, at its widest, AD 117** | Its extent |
| **The Roman provinces, AD 200** | The provinces, as dashed lines |
| **Shores of the archaic age, about 750–480 BC** | The shores of that age |
| **Shores of the classical age, 480–323 BC** | The shores of that age |
| **Shores of the Hellenistic age, 323–31 BC** | The shores of that age |
| **Shores of the Roman age, 31 BC to AD 300** | The shores of that age |

An extent is a purple tint with a dashed edge. A shore of an age shows water, with a dashed lapis edge, where there is land now. For example, the gulf at Miletus and the harbor of Ephesus. The dates of the layers are approximate. This device keeps your choice.

## Journeys

The app has these journeys:

- Paul's first, second and third journeys, and his voyage to Rome.
- Abraham's journey.
- Jacob's journey to Haran and back.
- The Exodus.
- Jesus' last journey to Jerusalem.

Each stop of a journey has the verse that names it.

### Draw a journey

1. Show the person in the search panel, such as Paul. Or open the guide of a passage that tells part of a journey.
2. Find **Journeys on the map** in the panel.
3. Tap a journey.

The map draws the route in ochre. A leg by land is a solid line, and a leg by sea is dashed. An arrow on each leg shows the direction, and a ring marks the start. A label in the corner of the map names the journey. Tap its **×** to take the route away.

### Routes that scholars propose

For some journeys, scholars propose more than one route. The panel lists each route, with what it rests on. Tap one to draw it. For example, the Exodus has a southern, a northern, a central and an Arabian route.

A proposed site that is not in the place data, such as one of the proposed sites of Mount Sinai, is an ochre ring with its name in italics. Its place is approximate.

Where Acts names only a region, such as Macedonia, the route goes to the usual city and marks it as a reading. The dates of the journeys are by Jones's chronology, as on the timeline.

## The scale

A scale bar in the corner of the map gives the distance. It changes as you zoom.
